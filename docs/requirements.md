# 6 Requirements Specification

## 6.1 Baseline and verification conventions

The baseline contains 22 functional and 12 non-functional requirements: 31 Must, one Should and two Could. Must is a release gate; Should is an explicit usability target; Could is conditional and requires its own test if committed. All numerical thresholds are proposed engineering acceptance targets, not university regulations or measured results. All production requirements remain unverified.

A denotes automated testing, I inspection/demonstration, R academic/source review and U user testing. AT identifies functional tests and NT quality tests. D1–D9 identify the frozen datasets defined in Chapter 9. Record build, inputs, versions, expected/actual results and evidence for every applicable criterion. No mandatory check passes merely because it is represented in a mockup.

## 6.4 Interpretation and acceptance decision

FR03 covers supported text-based PDFs up to the declared limit, with manual entry for image-only or unsupported layouts; actual AITU exports must be checked. Ten real assessment courses do not replace programme-level audit coverage of the official inventory. Missing curricula block a full-coverage claim. Mock documents can demonstrate interaction but cannot establish institutional applicability.

The validator checks a supplied plan; the generator searches for candidates and must call validation before publication. A timeout or no solution within a bounded horizon is not proof that graduation is globally impossible. Unknown policies remain unresolved. Hypothetical grades and predicted grades never become confirmed completions. All 31 Must requirements and no unresolved critical/major defects are required for baseline acceptance; any reduced scope requires a revised agreement before evaluation.
